What this models, and what it can't

Each formula is implemented from the office's own published description, linked on its card. Where a rule is ambiguous in the source, the card says so rather than inventing a number.

Three things no calculator can do for you: decide whether your school's "Honors" section is UC-certified or your dual-enrollment course is degree-level; know which of your courses sit on your state's approved list; or reproduce the reader who looks at your transcript next to your school's profile and never reduces it to one number at all. Highly selective private colleges mostly do that last thing.

Looked for, not modelled

Other formulas that really are published, and why they are not here:

  • South Carolina's Uniform Grading Policy (LIFE, Palmetto Fellows) — state-mandated, +0.5 honors and +1.0 AP, IB and dual credit. It runs on numeric grades out of 100, not letters, so this page cannot feed it without inventing a conversion.
  • Iowa's Regent Admission Index — published in full: (2 × ACT) + (class rank percentile) + (20 × GPA) + (5 × years of core courses), with 245 earning automatic admission. Needs a test score, a rank and a course count this page does not collect.
  • The University System of Georgia's Freshman Index — 500 × GPA + 1.06 × (SAT EBRW + Math) − 74. USG publishes the formula but not which GPA goes into it, and the choice changes the answer.
  • Cal Poly's Multi-Criteria Admission — last published in 2013 and withdrawn since. What circulates is reverse-engineered from admit patterns.
  • Harvard's reader ratings, surfaced in the SFFA litigation — a 1-to-6 judgement per applicant rather than arithmetic, and the reading procedures were largely redacted.

Modelling choices

  • A row holds one or two semester grades. A year grade counts as two equal semesters; UC and CSU cap honours points per semester, so a year-long AP earns two of them and a course with only an S1 mark earns one.
  • Plus and minus grades are used only in the baseline rows. Every recalculation here discards them, as their published rules specify.
  • Set grades in hand to the last year you have marks for. Everything recomputes on that slice, and each card carries its running value at every earlier year.
  • A card marked ★ unverified reflects a practice attributed to colleges that publish no formula. Every other card links to the office's own rule.
  • A formula whose window has not closed is marked provisional. UC's window is 10th–11th, so a GPA computed after 9th grade is not a UC GPA at all.
  • Course-count requirements (UF's 16 academic units, Bright Futures' 16 credits, NCAA's distribution rule) are not checked — only the GPA arithmetic is.
